# Lessons of the 2008 Financial Crisis and the Regulatory Response of Dodd-Frank and Basel III

**ECON 311 Honors Thesis · University of Massachusetts Amherst · Prof. Hans Despain · May 2025**
**Author:** Akanksha Sharma

## Summary
An analysis of what caused the 2008 financial crisis, how regulators responded, and where the system remains exposed.

## What the paper covers
- **Causes:** low interest rates, subprime and no-documentation lending, securitization into mortgage-backed securities and CDOs, high leverage, and lightly regulated shadow banks
- **Collapse:** Bear Stearns, Lehman Brothers, and the freeze in interbank and commercial paper markets
- **Response:** the Financial Stability Oversight Council, supervisory stress tests, the Volcker Rule, orderly liquidation authority, and Basel III capital and liquidity standards
- **Test case:** the 2023 failure of Silicon Valley Bank, linked to the 2018 rollback that exempted banks under $250B from stricter stress testing and liquidity rules
- **Emerging risks:** collateralized loan obligations, non-bank and fintech lenders, climate risk, and cyber risk

## Conclusion
Post-crisis reforms made banks safer, but regulation must keep adapting as financial innovation and political pressure create new gaps.
